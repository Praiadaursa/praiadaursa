Sacred Birman health What we test for

Sacred Birmans are a robust breed, but like every pedigree cat they carry hereditary risks. We name them openly and test for them rather than hope.

HCM — heart muscle disease

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is the most common feline heart disease and is documented in Birmans. In this breed a genetic test covers only part of the picture. That is why the parents of our breeding cats were examined by cardiac ultrasound with a cardiologist, as the breeding rules require.

PKD — polycystic kidney disease

Reliably detectable by genetic test. We breed exclusively with cats that have tested negative.

FeLV and FIV

Feline leukaemia and feline immunodeficiency virus are ruled out in both parents before every mating.

Blood group

Birmans occur in blood groups A and B. Mating a group A male with a group B female can be fatal for the kittens. We therefore know the blood group of both parents before mating.

Vaccination and deworming

Cat flu and feline panleukopenia are given as a first vaccination; the second dose follows after the kitten has moved out. Deworming is repeated according to the vet's schedule.

Diet

We feed high-quality wet food with a high meat content and no added sugar. Kittens leave with a feeding recommendation so that a change of diet does not coincide with the move.

Association

Felidae e.V.

We breed under the breeding and registration rules of Felidae e.V.. Every kitten leaves us with a recognised pedigree.
